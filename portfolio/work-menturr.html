Menturr

A two-sided mentoring platform for CodeDr — where people looking to be guided meet people running mentoring as work.

At a glance
RoleSole product designer
ClientCodeDr
ProcessWeekly reviews with the founder
Statusv1 shipped, app in build
Overview

Mentees wanted someone to hold their hand. Mentors wanted a business to run. Menturr had to be both at once.

CodeDr, a client I work with on and off, brought me in to design a platform connecting mentors with people who want mentoring. I was the sole designer, working from research through to handoff, with weekly calls with the founder to review work and collect feedback.

The brief sounded like a matching problem. It turned out to be a problem of two audiences whose definitions of a good session had almost nothing in common.

Research

Five interviews and a teardown of comparable platforms. A small sample, so I treated it as directional rather than conclusive — enough to find the shape of the problem, not enough to settle details on its own.

The interviews split cleanly by side of the marketplace, and that split became the thing the design had to solve.

The problem

Mentees weren't shopping for expertise. They wanted someone to walk them through something — hand-holding, in their words. That meant finding a person who fit their goals, booking time without friction whether one-to-one or in a group, and leaving with materials they could work from afterwards.

Mentors were running a side business. Mentoring sat alongside their own projects, so they needed to manage sessions, keep track of the people they were mentoring, organise materials, and earn from it — without the admin swallowing the time they were selling.

One product, two people with opposite relationships to the same session: one wants it to feel personal, the other needs it to be manageable at volume.

Decisions
  • Two dashboards, not one product with a toggle. The mentee's home is oriented around what's next and what to learn; the mentor's is oriented around load, mentees and earnings. Sharing one surface would have made both worse.
  • Booking carries the session type. One-to-one and group are chosen at booking rather than being separate products, which keeps the mentee's path identical either way and lets mentors run groups without managing a second workflow.
  • Materials attach to sessions. Tying resources to the session they belong to gave mentees the thing they leave with and mentors somewhere obvious to put it, rather than a library nobody maintains.
  • Add one thing you considered and rejected, and why. Even a small one — a layout you tried, a feature the founder wanted that you argued down. This is the item interviewers ask about.
Outcome

Shipped as v1. The landing page is live and the web app is in development.
