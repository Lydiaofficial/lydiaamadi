OpenRefine Reconciliation Dialog

Redesigning the reconciliation flow in an open-source data-cleaning tool used by researchers, librarians and journalists.

At a glance
RoleProduct Designer (contract)
ContextPost-Outreachy engagement
TeamWith OpenRefine maintainers
Year2024
Overview

Reconciliation is the step that decides whether a dataset is trustworthy — and it was the step new users abandoned.

OpenRefine is a free tool for cleaning and transforming messy datasets. Reconciliation, the process of matching data entities against an external service, is where accuracy is won or lost.

I was contracted immediately after my Outreachy internship to redesign the interface and experience of that process. The work ran across five areas: service selection, column mappings, results display, language handling, and preview placement.

The problem

User interviews surfaced a consistent pattern: people could not tell what the dialog was asking them to do. Terminology assumed familiarity with entity identifiers and property mappings that new users did not have.

The results view compounded it. Selecting a service returned far more candidates than anyone could evaluate, and expanded cells introduced whitespace that pushed the matching controls out of reach.

Decisions
  • Cut before adding. The sidebar and price icon were removed rather than restyled, and services shown in full view with their URLs. Less to interpret beats clearer labelling on something users touch once per dataset.
  • Sequence by scope. In column mappings, settings affecting the whole reconciliation sit above the line; constraints affecting results sit below. The hierarchy encodes what each control actually reaches.
  • Default to three, not all. Results cap at three candidates with an explicit reveal. Reducing the default was a smaller change than building filtering, and it addressed the interview finding directly.
  • Guard the irreversible. A confirmation before service deletion, and a hover prompt preventing users advancing without a selection.
