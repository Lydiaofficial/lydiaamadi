Kirgawa

An event management, ticketing and analytics web app with sponsorship built into the product rather than bolted on beside it.

At a glance
RoleSole UI/UX designer, from inception
CompanyEmmsDan Industries
Duration2022 – early 2024
ScopeEnd-to-end web app
Overview

Every event tool helps you sell tickets. Far fewer help you handle the people paying for the stage.

Kirgawa was my first full project in my first design job. I joined at inception as the only designer and stayed with the product for close to two years, which meant designing it, watching it get built, and living with the decisions long enough to see which ones held.

The brief was an end-to-end platform: create an event, manage attendees, sell tickets, read the analytics — and handle sponsors, which is the part most event software leaves to spreadsheets and email.

The problem

Event planning had no single place where creation, attendee management and analytics met. Organisers stitched the job together across tools, and the seams between them were where work leaked — details re-entered, numbers that didn't agree, no live read on how an event was actually going.

Sponsorship sat furthest outside the software. It's how many events are funded, and it was being run entirely on documents and inboxes.

Worth confirming: was the sponsorship focus something you found in research, or a bet the company made? Both are fine answers — but "we bet on it" and "users told us" are different claims and an interviewer will ask which.

Decisions
  • Onboarding ends at a created event, not a completed profile. Registration runs straight through to a first event so the product proves itself before it asks for commitment.
  • Event creation asks for the minimum. Name, date, time, and a generated link for virtual meetings. Everything else — branding, registration forms, agendas — is available afterwards rather than demanded upfront, so the first attempt can't stall.
  • Sponsorship packages as a real object. Rather than a logo slot on the event page, organisers build packages they can offer. That turns sponsorship from decoration into something the platform can actually help sell.
  • Empty states designed, not left over. A new organiser sees an empty product on day one, so those screens got the same attention as the full ones.
Outcome

Did it launch? Is it live at kirgawa.com now? Any number at all — events created, organisers signed up, tickets sold — or plainly "built and launched in 2023, since discontinued." Anything specific beats leaving this blank.

Looking back

Two or three sentences. This was your first product and you had no senior designer to check your work — say what you'd approach differently now. Distance from your earliest work is the clearest evidence of growth you can show, and it's a question you will be asked.
